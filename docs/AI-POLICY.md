# AI design policy

Binding for every AI behavior change. Goal: an AI that is **functional, predictable and hard to exploit**, built from a few general mechanisms instead of many special cases. Read before writing a rule; check the rule against the checklist at the end.

## 1. Principles

1. **Evidence first.** Add or change a rule only for a failure seen in a log (`mod log`, L marks) or proven in code. State the failure and the expected log change up front. No speculative features.
2. **Steer vanilla, don't replace it.** Vanilla already has gauges → actions → orders (phases, priorities, fight power checks, retreat). We fix its inputs (threat estimates, targets, force size) and fill gaps (army hunting, contesting). We rewrite a subsystem only when hooking inputs can't express the fix.
3. **One world model, one power metric.** Every decision reads the same per-faction threat picture (§3), with power from `HPowerScore.singlePowerScore(HCombatStats.unitCombatStats)`. No rule scans the world with its own radius or formula.
4. **Plans are commitments.** A decision creates a plan (§4) that holds until one of its declared triggers fires. No re-deciding every tick, and no flip-flopping between two options.
5. **Concentrate force.** Send enough to win with margin, all together. Never trickle armies in one by one. If we can't gather enough, don't go.
6. **Every army has exactly one owner.** An army belongs to one order. Only a higher priority on the ladder (§5) may take it. Taking one army from an order still gathering cancels the whole order, so a siege launch never takes from another gathering multi-army Military order (vanilla's Annex prio 3 > Liberate / Pillage 1 is not a ladder step); a Defense may, it needs them. Rules never issue raw unit commands behind an order's back.
7. **Fail safe.** Injected code runs in traps. If our logic fails or finds nothing, vanilla behavior continues unchanged.
8. **Observable.** Every decision logs one event with its inputs, the choice and a reason code, so `mod log` can show why.
9. **Small complexity budget.** Prefer one general mechanism (e.g. "local power ratio with hysteresis") over N special cases. Delete rules that don't measurably help.

## 2. Decision layers

| Layer | Scope | Cadence | Who decides | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic | faction | aware scan, posture held ≥ 60 s (§5b) | director (§5b, design) over vanilla gauges, aggressiveness and diplomatic targets | defend / recover / press E / expand / harass, which soft target |
| Operational | army group ↔ one target | aware scan (10 s), acts **only on triggers** | vanilla orders + our rules (`hunt`, contest, abort) | take village X, hunt army Y, retreat to Z |
| Tactical | units in one fight | per tick | vanilla `unitMicroManagement` | focus fire, reposition, flee |

A layer may constrain the one below it, never micromanage it. Operational rules don't touch unit micro; tactical micro doesn't pick targets.

## 3. World model (per faction, refreshed every aware scan)

This is the only input for operational decisions:
- **Hostile groups:** at-war armies clustered by proximity (250). For each group: power, position, heading/moving, visible, on its own territory, supply state, and intent when known (`aiOrder`).
- **Own groups:** position, power, life/supply, owner order, free/busy.
- **Threat at a point:** `threat(p, r)` = hostile army power within r of p (an army occupying a structure > 60 from p counts 25% for p on our land, 50% off it: busy capturing elsewhere, but it can break off against an offensive; for p on our land an idle army off our land counts 50%: it must walk in first; both full when p is their own position or capture; none of these discounts for where to heal / retreat / send an idle army home: an army rests there, next to a stack that is free once its capture ends) (all at-war factions and neutral raiders whose raid targets us, plus enemy structure defenses when relevant; movers count only while heading towards p). `strength(p, r)` = own power that can arrive in time.
- **ETA:** distance divided by speed (approximate). "Can arrive before X" beats raw radius.

**Diplomacy:** combat is allowed only at `War` (`State.areAtWar`). Truce (`Peace`), Tribute and Alliance forbid it: fighting breaks the relationship. `Hostility` never fights. So threat = at-war armies only, read **live** on every query. A war declaration or a truce is therefore a trigger for every running plan at the next scan: an attack on a new truce partner is cancelled, and a new enemy's armies count at once. Truce partners are *potential* threats (they can declare war) but count 0 for now. Offensive vs defensive posture: later, from the strategic layer (aggressiveness), e.g. whether hunts may enter enemy home territory.

Implemented queries (tools/rules/world.py, every rule must use them): `aimod_threat(fac, p, r)` (at-war + raiders-targeting-us power within r plus moving armies heading to p and able to arrive within 20 s; speed ≈ 6 units/s, so ≈ 120 units), `aimod_neutral` (raiders busy with someone else: they fight back when hunted), `aimod_threat_now` (3 s horizon), `aimod_react` (+ free at-war armies within REACT_R: raid), `aimod_danger(fac, zone)` (place memory, §3a), `aimod_hthreat` (hunt: around the prey's faction; a third party at war with the prey is skipped, one allied / at peace with it counts only within our nearest army's distance + ALLY_WIN 150, i.e. if it arrives before the kill), `aimod_own(fac, p, r, exclude)`, `aimod_terrain(fac, zone)`, `aimod_land(fac, e)` (distance to our land), `aimod_supok` / `aimod_short` (supply budget), `aimod_sieged(fac, s)` (siege by an at-war faction, from the structure), `aimod_cover(fac, e, exclude, own, res)` (turret cover at e, §5a), `aimod_defend(fac)` (our besieged structure). `threat` itself is armies only; rules add `aimod_cover` where structures matter. State between scans: added-global maps (`_global_map`), not saved with the game. Not built: the persisted per-faction snapshot; village militia in hunt threat.

### 3a. Intel: what an AI may know (first step built)

**Built: place memory (`rules/memory.py`).** Per faction, zone → time of the last danger event there; `aimod_danger(fac, zone)` = 1 right after it, fading linearly to 0 over DANGER_T (180 s): a cooldown, so a place is avoided for a while, then tried again. Events: our harvester attacked there. Consumers: harvesting-team field choice (key + danger × 600²: a field up to ~600 farther wins). Growth path, one step at a time and each from a logged failure: more event kinds (army lost, siege failed, raid seen) with their own weight and decay; spreading to neighbour zones; more consumers (siege targets, Discovery trips, harvester recall); the army memory below. Every rule reads the same `aimod_danger`; no private memories.

Today both vanilla (`cacheData.totalArmyPowerScores` in aggressiveness and diplomacy) and our `aimod_threat` read **true** enemy strength, seen or not. Target: decisions use what the faction has seen.
- **Memory per enemy army:** last seen position, power, time (refreshed whenever `isVisibleForFaction`). Seen dying → removed.
- **Position confidence decays:** an unseen army may be anywhere within `last_pos + speed × age` (capped). Local threat counts it only if that circle reaches the query point, and at a weight that falls with age.
- **Strength confidence decays slower:** armies don't vanish. Perceived faction strength = Σ remembered power, with old entries fading toward a floor (e.g. 50% after 5 min, never 0 while the faction lives). Unknown growth is estimated from the enemy's territory size.
- **Result:** `perceived(F)` = perceived total strength of faction F; `threat()` and the balance below use memory, not omniscience. Scouting (ornithopters, contact) gains real value.
- **Storage:** needs state kept between scans (a new global, or a field on an existing per-faction object); not persisted in saves, rebuilt after load (conservative until re-seen).

### 3b. Diplomacy: war goals and who is in charge (design, not built)

Peace, truce and war are strategic plans with the same commitment rules as orders (§4).
- **Balance:** `B(F) = our strength / perceived(F)`. `B ≥ 1.5` = we're in charge; `B ≤ 0.67` = they are; in between = contested.
- **Accepting peace (`tradeRequestReceived` gate):** refuse a peace offer from F while any of these holds:
  1. we have an order against F in Engage/Action with local ratio ≥ `keep` (finish the capture or pillage first; decide on peace after it completes). **Built** (`rules/peace.py`, `peace-gate`): Military orders on F's structures only (sieges; hunts on F's armies not yet), any offer from at-war F carrying a treaty, and "ratio ≥ keep" read as "the order is still in Engage/Action" (vanilla's fight retreat cancels a losing siege). Not covered: our own peace offers to F mid-siege (vanilla `Trading.doTrade`);
  2. we're in charge (`B ≥ 1.5`) and the offer doesn't pay more than what we'd take by force (at least: not while we can still annex or pillage their villages near us).

  Accept when contested or losing, when we have nothing to gain nearby, or when the offer is worth more than continuing.
- **Offering peace:** the weaker side offers when losing (`B ≤ 0.67`) or when a stronger third enemy appears. Offering peace is not a substitute for defending: a faction under attack still defends.
- **Declaring war:** only with a goal (a reachable target and `B ≥ enter` locally), not by mood.
- **Hysteresis:** after a status change, no reversal for a minimum time unless the balance swings strongly (a new trigger), same as §4.

Vanilla today: daily `desiredStatus` (0 peace / 1 war / 2 total war) from `HScoring.diplomaticTarget` (hegemony, CHOAM, AI type, true army totals / distance). Offers a peace pact when at war and status 0 (random chance `valueCache[279]`). Accepts one unless status 2, by trade value only, with no look at running sieges (a winning capture gets cancelled by the peace).

## 4. Plans, triggers, hysteresis

A plan = `{goal, target, armies, ratio_at_commit, keep, abort, expiry}`. In practice that is a vanilla `AIOrder` plus our thresholds.

**Ratios (one scale for all rules):**

| Threshold | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `enter` | 1.5 | start an offensive plan only at ≥ this local ratio (ours / all hostile) |
| `keep` | 1.0 | continue while projected ratio ≥ this |
| `abort` | 0.7 | below this, re-decide: an open-field fight 1-2 armies down is kept (operations can swing it) |
| `retreat` | 0.65 | vanilla fight retreat threshold (data `AI_WarzonePowerEstimation_RetreatRatio`, fixed; vanilla re-rolls a range every tick). The balance 1.0 that means "no enemy power" is never multiplied |
| terrain | ×1.3 own zone, ×0.8 at-war zone | multiplies our ratio before any enter/abort/retreat test: at home we heal and resupply, on their land they do. Attack on their land needs 1.5/0.8 ≈ 1.9×; home defense holds to ≈ 0.5 |
| `flee ETA` | 3 s / contact 80 | an army at its own healing structure (≤ 120) leaves only if hostiles within 80 or 3 s of it overwhelm it (> 2 × 1.3 × ours): last resort |
| hunt gap | 30 s | one hunt start per faction per 30 s (commit; also covers vanilla cancelling a fresh order) |
| detour | 250 | a contested healing structure costs this much extra distance; only an overwhelming threat rules it out |
| `commit` | 30 s | after a switch, no switch back unless a **new** trigger makes things worse |
| supply budget | 0.35 × distance to our land + 10% max supply | supply an army needs to walk home (drain ≈ 0.28/unit, ×1.25 margin). `short` = losing supply and below it: hunts abort (in contact too, unless on the prey with `KILL` × its threat: finishing it takes seconds; never for a prey without power, e.g. a harvester: it takes minutes), and in a fight the balance is × (1 − 0.6 × short share of our power), so a starving army leaves any fight it isn't clearly winning. Joining a hunt needs 1.25 × the budget at the target (hysteresis). **Recall:** when every army of ours in a fight is short and none has a Military order (the hunt / siege that took it there ended), the fight balance is 0: it leaves at once, even when winning (pursuing a fleeing enemy gains nothing for a starving army). **Stranded:** a short army whose supply can't pay the walk home (< 0.28 × distance to our land) gets neither penalty nor recall: withdrawing is impossible (§4 choice 4), so the plain terrain-adjusted balance decides and a won fight is finished |
| our land | structures on zones we own | every "distance to our structures" test; structures inside other factions' zones (UWHeadquarters) don't count |

The gap enter > keep > abort is the hysteresis that stops flip-flopping. Entry and exit must measure the same thing at the same place (hunt: the prey at start, the group's core = the member nearest our land afterwards; never a centroid, which a member walking home drags under its own guns); otherwise a plan can start and abort on consecutive checks. Members more than LOCAL from the core have split off and leave the target group.

**Deep desert:** supply drains several times faster there; a chase or neutral hunt whose prey stands in a `DeepDesert` zone starts only in contact (≤ CONTACT) with KILL × (as `far`), and a running one aborts `desert` once the prey's core member is there, unless we are on it with KILL ×.

**Annex value:** when the best pick can't be paid now, the best affordable one of the top 3 goes instead (one unaffordable pick stalled the Annex while other gauges spent the armies); none affordable: wait for the best. A Liberate / Raze of a weak enemy village is fine; its cost is the trip, judged at launch only (no flip-flop): it waits while our Annex is pending (better work at home, beyond LOCAL), and it doesn't go when the at-war armies that could reach our land before we are back (more of them the farther it is) × ENTER exceed half our army there × OWN_T. The director's pressed village is exempt. Which village to take is value per cost: vanilla's score (distance, resource fields, region, main-base proximity, owned neighbours) + 30 for a special region (hegemony), + 10 for a zone with a sietch, × compactness (nearest own structure: 1.25 next to it, 0.75 from 800 away; not for factions without distance annex costs, i.e. Smugglers, who cap far villages by design), × 1.3 for a bunker pair, × cheapest / its Authority cost (a village costing two counts half). Opening: while we own no spice village, spice villages × 2 (vanilla +100 on top), except Fremen / Vernius (they harvest anywhere: vanilla's +100 is removed for them). Raids never take any of vanilla's top Annex picks (2-5 by difficulty). **Fremen deep desert:** a deep desert becomes Fremen land once every neighbouring village (and main base) zone is theirs (needs their first hegemony bonus). Villages around an unowned deep desert score × (1 + Σ chance / (1 + villages still missing)) (completing one ×2, one away ×1.5, cap ×3) plus a flat DD_ADD 40 × the best chance (per cost, like the special +30: the multiplier alone is ~×1 on big rings), full weight from the start (before the bonus too: the opening otherwise took a special and a spice village before the ring villages next door). Stall guard: each missing village owned by another faction or within 350 of another faction's main base halves the chance; a desert bordered by another faction's main base is impossible (no bonus), so the AI rings the deserts it can finish instead of feeding a contested one. **One ring at a time:** the ringable desert (unowned, next to a village, no other faction's main base bordering it, every missing village neutral: a held one stalls it) fewest zone hops from our main base is the focus (same hops: the one with the fewest villages still missing); every farther desert's chance × DD_FAR 0.5 (two near deserts were capped in parallel, both half-done). **Ring first:** once Fremen have the hegemony bonus that makes a ring pay, while an uncontested ring is in progress and close (one neighbour already ours, at most 2 villages missing besides the candidate, every missing village neutral and > 350 from enemy main bases, no enemy main base on it) and its village is an Annex candidate or under our Annex, Fremen annex nothing off the ring: those villages are pillaged instead (a spice village is worth little to Fremen, who harvest anywhere). It ends when the ring is contested or after 3 launches on that village (two failed: resistance), and then the off-ring village is fine to take. Neither raid nor a pillage press ever hits an uncontested ring village (any size, bonus or not). The director's annex-mode press is never dropped by the hold (its call stands); the bunker redirect (village next to our main base) also goes first.

**Worn armies heal:** an army under 50% life joins no vanilla mission (siege, Defense, Discovery) unless it is already fighting where it stands; vanilla's 90% floor applies only to armies with safe regeneration. Exception: worn temporary units (no regeneration, they disband soon) still defend our structures, as extras on top of a Defense the healthy armies already carry (and in main-base defenses), never counted toward its required power. Hunts take ≥ 90%, raids ≥ 60%.

**Deep desert at a target:** (Engage / Action only: never a lone step ahead of the pack in Regroup, never back to the village in Retreat) an army of a siege or raid that stands in the deep desert near the target (draining: not occupying) steps onto the village's side, 40 from it, and keeps fighting there: no retreat, just off the multiplied drain. Fremen ring villages that touch none of our land except deep desert get half the ring bonus (reaching them means crossing desert; the pathfinder already charges deep desert +3× its distance).

**Worms (reaction, not prediction):** no worm-risk model in target choice. A worm-targeted army of ours on sand drops its order and walks to the nearest rock at once (its order's other armies on sand within 150 of the worm too); armies moved wait on the rock while a worm is within 150 (at most 20 s): no hunt / raid, no vanilla Resupply or mission re-order back over the sand (Atreides retreating from Adur stopped and restarted every 3-4 s for 70 s that way, losing supply). The others of the order get a new order at once and keep going. A moved harvester marks its zone as danger, so the team re-route sends it to another field instead of back onto the worm's sand.

**Harvester under fire:** an attacked harvester whose escort within 150 is weaker than the attackers runs at once to the nearest safe spice field (outside its zone, no danger memory, no hostile power within 250, not another harvester's), else home; vanilla's re-route leaves it alone for 20 s; it doesn't wait for vanilla's team re-route, which keeps it deployed when no other free field is near.

**Hunt awareness:** "could come" counts at-war armies within 480 that are capturing a village at half power (they break off: Fremen left Ars-ha to answer); the hunt's first Engage target is the armed enemy nearest the anchor, a powerless one (harvester) only when the group has no armed member: fight the escort in front, not a far weak target past it. Vanilla only recalls `ent.Harvester` units.

**Force size:** concentrate, but not everything: a hunt takes the nearest free armies until HUNT_TO 2.5 × their side (prey + enemy armies free to come within REACT_R, + cover) / terrain (KILL for a restricted start), and past HUNT_CAP 2 × prey group size + 1 armies it stops as soon as the entry ratio holds (Smugglers sent 8 armies at one H_Soldier). Contests of our besieged villages take armies until HUNT_TO whatever the count (no cap, every free army in reach if short; armies alone fill it, turrets don't; goal 2): Harkonnen sent all 21 armies 540 to a 29k besieger at Eyron while 850k Fremen closed on Qafiel, where they stood. The chosen force must still have ENTER × that side (KILL restricted), else no hunt (the evaluation saw only the prey's threat). Raids: RAID_TO; sieges: JOIN_TO.

**Gathering:** concentrate, but don't idle next to the prey, and never wait in the deep desert while draining (desert-step moves that army onto the village's side instead), and never wait for a hunted army that is moving away (it escapes).

**Siege position:** capture from the side of the village away from enemy guns. A siege army (Action: within 250 of the target; Engage: within 80) under at-war turret cover other than the target's steps to 25 from the village centre on the far side from the nearest enemy structure (still capturing), at most every 4 s, which also pulls back armies micro chased under the guns. Vanilla walks to the nearest edge and chases defenders anywhere (Fremen capped Sadnin from the side facing Arrakeen and lost 6 of 10 armies; the one army on the far side stayed at full health). **Retreat target:** a fight retreat keeps the heal structure it picked first for HEAL_COMMIT 15 s (vanilla re-issues it every tick; the target flipped Ur-Al'nun / Qartsan / Arkdad and 2 Fremen armies died at Qartsan). On our land, a structure on the enemy's side of the fight costs + DETOUR²: retreat deeper into our land, not into the pursuers' path; off our land home stays the goal. **Lone candidate:** past the opening (we own more than EARLY_VILLAGES 2 structures), when vanilla offers a single Annex candidate (supply range still small) it is taken only if it adds something: a deep-desert ring, a special region or spice; else it is dropped and the armies stay free (Fremen took Ur-Al'nun, plain, 181 from Tabr, 30 s before ring villages entered range). The director's pressed village is exempt. **Keep capture:** vanilla micro gives the first army of a fight group (any life, any distance) the role 'defend our nearest structure that has a besieger or occupier flag' and Repositions it there; an army within 60 of a village we besiege or occupy never takes that role (Fremen F_Sneak left the Tabwan capture for Altar, 130 away, with no enemy at Altar, and walked back). Defense of that structure stays with orders (Defense, contest hunt, rally). **Unstick:** a siege army in Action within 30 of the target that hasn't moved 3 in 12 s while the target is neither besieged nor occupied walks to the target's `getSafePosition` (vanilla re-sends `ArmySiege` every tick and never moves an army already in occupation range: a Fremen F_Sneak stood 4 from Tabwan's centre for 2.5 min, Idle/Attack every 0.5 s, no siege, until a second army came). Main-base guns count 3× in every power measure (ours and vanilla's sizing): their power score badly understates them.

**Rally (gather strength):** when one of our structures is actively threatened (an at-war army at it or heading there: a stack idling at its own border is a standoff, not an attack; Smugglers walked 8 armies Shariyah ↔ Tuek every 30-60 s for Atreides armies standing at Hadnin) and faces more at-war power (≥ 80k, counting armies that can come) than all our defenders within 600 plus our turrets there, × home terrain, can beat by 1.5, nobody fights it piecemeal: every defender not in contact walks to one rally point (the main base if no enemy is within 150 of it, else our nearest structure ≥ 150 from the danger and clear of enemies), its Resupply / Defense / Patrol order dropped and vanilla kept from re-sending it; structures within 150 of the danger aren't used to heal or flee to. Once the gathered force passes the ratio (×1.15 to end, no flicker), the contest hunt (village besieged) or vanilla's Defense takes them in together: a decisive defense. No rally when the defenders already stand at the danger (within LOCAL) with at least even force there (+ turrets, × home terrain): they fight it now, the rest walks in; gathering elsewhere walks a gathered army away and back (Harkonnen's 21 armies at Qafiel went 530 to Carthag and returned in three waves). Atreides at Aeganim had fed 1-2 armies at a time into 10 Fremen (fight, flee 26 to Sadnin, heal, fight) while 4 idled.

**Disengage:** an army sent to heal (Resupply) that is still shooting something without power (a harvester, an empty structure) is pulled out by vanilla's retreat at once, unless a hunt / siege / raid of ours is fighting there. Vanilla Regroup only waits for each army at its own path's last waypoint, so armies from different sides engage 60-120 apart and the nearest fights alone. In Engage, a leader more than 15 ahead of the farthest army of the attack (within 400 of the target; farther ones are stragglers) waits where it is, out of contact (> 80 from the target, not fighting), at most 10 s per attack: a few seconds of waiting for a fight in force. A hunt waits in Regroup only until the armies already within LOCAL of the core pass `enter` there; then all engage and the stragglers walk straight in (they reinforce a fight already won on paper, which is not trickling). Same for every siege order of ours (`siege-engage`: armies within ENGAGE_R 100 of the target, i.e. already at it, vs threat + enemy cover + militia, `enter`, 1.25 on neutral targets; armies strung out on the way would arrive one by one). While part of the order is still on the way, the armies at the target need PART_X 2 × that, else they wait and the test repeats as the rest arrives (Fremen's 2 of 3 on Haykus started at 2.4× by our count, vanilla's fight balance read 1.5, and lost an army). Siege picks don't pull armies off Discovery trips farther than JOIN_R from the target. Every siege judgment (launch sizing, engage, raid, press) counts neutral raiders at the village that aren't aimed at us (renegades, other factions' raiders) with its militia: they fight whoever contests it.

**No thinking loops:** a target vanilla keeps picking but can't launch on (3 orderless launches in 120 s while we had an army to send: ArmyNotStrongEnough, NotEnoughArmies, ...; no free army at all, e.g. wiped and respawning, never counts) leaves the target list for 120 s, doubling to 480 s while the loop comes back; a launch that ends at once is blocked 30 s doubling to 240 s. The armies are free meanwhile for raids, hunts and other targets (Fremen's 10 armies sat at Ars-bu for minutes while the gauges re-picked Harkonnen's Aliftah every ~25 s).

**Distance and objectives:** when several targets pass `enter`, prefer objectives (a village near our land being besieged by an enemy, read from the village's siege state: weight 3) and nearness: score = weight × threat / (distance of our nearest free army + 100). A plan anchored on an objective ends when the fight drifts away from our land (`drift`), not by chasing. A chase yields to an objective: when its own armies could contest a siege near our land, it aborts (`objective`) and only contests may start for 30 s. Chasing far from our land gains nothing: the supply budget ends it before the way home becomes unaffordable, and a hunt starts only when the trip (walk out + way back) fits the budget. A place where a hunt just failed (supply, weak, turret, desert) is left alone for a minute: no run out and back at the next target there. A prey running on its own land is followed only while one of its members is nearly dead (life < 0.35): finishing a dying unit under their turrets is worth it, chasing full-health runners home isn't (chip damage would keep counting as progress); a prey standing to fight is finished. A prey running home under its main base: that base's defense weighs into the threat from 250 away (× 1 − d/250, soft: a stack that really kills there still goes; judged the same when the hunt starts and while it runs), and the "we crush it" waiver of the supply abort lasts 10 s of the prey running at most, summed over the hunt (a prey standing and fighting is finished). After any hunt, a stack off our land facing an enemy worth < 10% of it, with no mission, leaves (`trivial`), even if stranded.

**Re-decide only on triggers**, never on a timer alone:
1. A new hostile group gets within threat range of our armies or target, and it can arrive before we finish.
2. The projected ratio crosses `abort`.
3. The target becomes invalid: dead, owner changed, invisible for > N s, or it entered the enemy's home territory (for hunts).
4. We are stuck: no progress for N s. For a chase: PURSUIT_T 15 s engaged without the enemy losing PROGRESS 10% power or us closing max(CLOSE 30, (gap − CATCH_R 40) × PURSUIT_T / CATCH_T 30) on it = it runs as fast as we do, or so little slower that catching it takes longer than CATCH_T (Harkonnen followed a raider 57 s / 230 units, 205 behind at 2 u/s) (hunt `chase`, which also bars the runners from new chases for GIVEUP_T 90 s; the fight retreat's `pursuit` for fights with no mission off our zone, its clock kept through missions so it acts as soon as the order ends).
5. The plan expires.

**On a trigger, choose in this order:**
1. **Continue** if the projected ratio (including the threat's ETA) is still ≥ `keep`.
2. **Reinforce** if free armies can arrive in time and lift the ratio to ≥ `keep`.
3. **Withdraw** to the nearest *safe* point (`safe-heal` logic) if we can get there before contact.
4. **Fight all-in** if withdrawing is impossible (they're faster or closer to our escape path), or the ratio is still ≥ `abort` and the objective is worth it (own structure, nearly done capture).
5. Otherwise withdraw anyway. Losing slowly is worse than retreating late.

Example: marching to capture a village, a large hostile army is spotted heading our way. That is trigger 1. We project the ratio at the village at their ETA, then work down the list: finish if the capture completes first and we still hold ≥ `keep`, pull in reinforcements, fall back, or fight if we're cornered.

## 5. Priority ladder (who may take an army from whom)

| Prio | Orders |
|---|---|
| 10 | defend main base (vanilla) |
| 5 | defense of own structure (vanilla), `hunt`, contest of a siege near us, neutral-raider hunt (only when no at-war chase/contest qualifies) |
| 4 | Raze |
| 3 | Annex (`siege-join`: required ≥ 1.25 on neutral targets (EARLY_REQ 1.0 = vanilla while we own fewer than EARLY_VILLAGES 2 villages: the opening's 2 armies must take the first villages, at launch and in `siege-engage`) (militia is known and never reinforced; the joiners add margin), and the nearest idle armies within 450 that pass the supply budget join the launch until we have 3 × (threat + enemy cover + militia) / terrain there: a short fight, the rest stays free for parallel captures; `annex-spacing`: no second siege within 100 of one we run; none at all while one of ours is besieged; bunker villages first; refusal = `Dismiss`, no gauge decay) |
| 1 | Pillage (vanilla gauge, and `raid`; a running pillage yields to `defend` / home threats, see §5a 5b), Liberate |
| 0 | Patrol, Resupply, Discovery (vanilla; a lone Discovery trip needs `enter` × the at-war threat at the event / terrain, else refused: `discovery-gate`; while it runs, the same test with movers DISC_HORIZON 40 s out (twice the launch horizon: leave with a lead) cancels it, the event is skipped DISC_RETRY 60 s, `strand` takes the army home); `strand` Patrol home (an idle army on hostile land) |

Rule: new rules pick priorities from this table and add themselves to it. An army in Resupply is never "free", whatever its priority: healing comes first. An army short of supply on neutral or enemy land starts nothing new and leaves fights it isn't clearly winning (supply budget, §4).

**Turret awareness (every goal).** Turrets (buildings with power, e.g. MissileBattery 40, range 80) and main base guns (power 80, range 80) cover COVER_R = 130 around their structure. Vanilla ignored every turret but the target's own and its owner's main base, and counted the target's own turret although it stops while the village is annexed or pillaged. Rules:
- a village under siege has silent turrets (its own attacker's view and the defender's view);
- attack sizing (vanilla `pickUnits`, live order balance) adds the turrets of every other at-war structure covering the target, and drops the target's own; defense sizing drops our besieged village's turrets and adds our covering neighbours'. Vanilla's formula (Σ offense × Σ HP) makes turrets feared in proportion to the armies they support: more, not overwhelmingly;
- hunts count cover as threat (turret offense × one army's HP ≈ one army per turret, two per main base) and our own cover as power. A chase under enemy cover is allowed only in contact (≤ 150) with ≥ 3 × (threat + cover): kill it, take a few shots, leave; it aborts (`turret`) otherwise. Attacking a main base itself is a separate future plan that needs overwhelming force.

## 5b. Strategic director: the bird's-eye view (steps 1-2 built: `rules/strat.py`)

One per-faction evaluation decides what the **spare** armies do, so the rules above stop deciding alone: goals 1-2 of §5a stay reflexes, the director orders everything from goal 3 down. Target: an AI that is hard to beat for a human because it punishes every exposure (undefended village, lone army, empty home) quickly, with overwhelming force, and never gives openings (kiting, turret baits, pull-aways).

**Why (vanilla, code-confirmed; details README / REVERSING):** an enemy village is an Annex candidate only if (a) aggressiveness ≥ 50, one faction-wide value driven by *total* army power vs the weighted average of all others, and (b) `Diplomacy.desiredStatus` toward its owner is ≥ 1, i.e. the owner is the daily *diplomatic* target. A faction at war with a weak neighbour whose diplomatic wish is peace never touches its villages. Nothing compares "take a weak enemy village next door" with "annex a far neutral one". Testbed match: aggressiveness 100 for three factions from 05:00-08:30, zero enemy villages targeted all match, `Annexation:Invalid` (no candidate) 59-140× per faction, no siege launched after 08:29 (neutral land used up): the mid game idles.

**Picture (every aware scan, from §3; no private scans):**
- *Spare force* S = power of our armies free for an offensive (the `raidable` set: not defending, not resupplying below RAID_LIFE) minus what home needs: ENTER × `aimod_home(fac, FRONT_R)` / OWN_T, held by the armies nearest home.
- *Front* per at-war faction E: E's villages within FRONT_R (600) of our land. hold(v) = `aimod_react` + enemy cover + militia at v (the raid measure). v is **soft** when the spare armies that can reach it have ≥ PRESS (3 = `KILL`) × hold(v) / terrain: the fight is over in seconds.
- *Balance* B(E) = our total power / E's (true today, perceived with §3a).

**Postures, first match wins** (each held ≥ COMMIT 60 s unless a higher one triggers; logged as `strat` rows):

| # | Posture | When | What the spare armies do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | DEFEND (built) | our structure besieged | contest, no new offensive |
| 2 | RECOVER | S ≤ 0, or more than half our power `short` or below RAID_LIFE | nothing new; heal and gather at home |
| 3 | PRESS E | a soft E village exists, B(E) ≥ 1 (never poke the stronger side), its utility ≥ the best neutral one | take it: Annex if affordable and holdable (supply range, not isolated), else pillage; exposed E armies near it are hunted on the way |
| 4 | EXPAND | neutral villages in reach and affordable | vanilla Annex (siege-join sizing) |
| 5 | HARASS | nothing above applies (e.g. Smugglers short of Authority, neutral land gone) | `raid` pillage, hunt exposed armies; never idle > 60 s with spare armies: patrol the front for vision |

**One target utility** (ranks neutral Annex, enemy Annex and pillage on one scale): U = value × swing / time. time = walk ETA of the force + fight (∝ hold / our power) + action duration (Annex 6 days, × `valueCache[512]` on neutral; Pillage 2 days). value = the village's production (+ `BUNKER_W` for bunker pairs); swing = 2 for an enemy village (we gain, they lose), 1 neutral; pillage = loot now + their 20-day production halving, minus our doubled Annex cost there. "Don't waste time unless overwhelming" = only soft villages (PRESS ×) qualify, and every PRESS siege gets an **expiry** of 1.5 × its estimated time: missed, or local ratio < `keep` (relief arrived), it aborts (`slow` / `weak`) and §4 decides.

**Anti-exploit rules (human opponents):** kiting → `chase` / `pursuit` (built); turret baits → cover (built); pull-away (lure the stack out, hit home) → home race on every offensive, not only raids; split raids → DEFEND takes the structure with the most value at risk first, no trickling; predictability → keep vanilla's random pick among the top `BestStructuresCount`; omniscience → §3a memory, so scouting and fog matter both ways.

**Levers (steer vanilla, §1.2):** the `Diplomacy.getTargetStatus` call in `getSiegeableVillages` → 1 for the PRESS faction; `tryAnnexation`'s `allowEnemy` → true while PRESS; the `HScoring.structures` wrapper → × U ratio, enemy villages that aren't soft removed; siege sizing to PRESS × on enemy targets (`siege-join`); an abort pass for our enemy sieges (expiry, `weak`, like `raid`'s); `raid` ranks by U instead of nearest.

**Built: RECOVER (step 5, simplified):** worn power W = our non-harvester armies with life < RAID_LIFE or `short`; RECOVER while W > RECOVER_IN 0.5 × T, left only below RECOVER_OUT 0.35 × T (hysteresis), checked before a new press (a running press is judged by its own keep test). Effects: no new press; `raid` launches only raids that are themselves a recovery (each raid army has the village nearer than our land, entry RAID_TO 3× instead of ENTER: a worn stack next to a weak village refills by pillaging instead of walking home). Not gated: hunts (they take only armies at ≥ 90% life/supply), vanilla sieges (same), defense.

**Built (steps 1-2), simplified:** posture 2 is `hold` (no spare force for a new target: busy, healing or needed at home; a running press is judged before it, since its armies aren't spare); press picks the *nearest* soft village (no utility ranking yet, and no comparison with the best neutral option: a soft enemy village within FRONT_R always wins over expanding); mode annex = in supply range + Annex available + affordable (`getOccupationActionCost` / `hasResources`), else pillage (via `raid`, which prefers it) if available; keep = our power within PRESS_R (any task) + cover ≥ PRESS_KEEP 0.7 × their side / terrain; drop on owner change (done / gone), Devastated (`canBeAttacked` false: done), truce, PRESS_MAX 240 s (`slow`), weak; a drop cancels our Military orders on it and skips it for PRESS_RETRY 120 s. While pressing E, vanilla's target lists keep only the pressed village among E's (Annex ×PRESS_W in annex mode, no Annex in pillage mode). Defend ends a press.

**Build order**, each step verified before the next: (1) observation: a `strat` row per faction and scan (S, home need, soft villages with hold and U, B per E, desiredStatus per E, chosen posture), no behaviour change; (2) PRESS unlock (gates + scores + sizing); (3) PRESS expiry / abort pass; (4) utility ranking in `raid` and the Annex scores; (5) RECOVER; (6) front patrols.

## 5c. Contact tension (steps 1-2 built; step 3 design)

**Failure:** Aid-Al'ha (neutral, then Fremen) and Qal-nit (Atreides) stand ~85 apart, one turret range. Fremen captured Aid-Al'ha unopposed while Atreides sat in `rally` (280-330k Fremen vs 410-450k Atreides, no contest); afterwards both raids refused the other's village every 30 s (`weak`). Contact points are worth more than their production: whoever holds both has a bunker pair and a safe border; leaving one to the other side is a permanent threat.

**One quantity, read by existing rules; nothing else changes at tension 0.**
- *Contact pair:* our village V and the nearest other-owned or neutral village W within CONTACT_R 140. Main bases excluded (the bunker rule covers them).
- *Tension* T(V) in [0, 1], updated in the strat scan (10 s): + T_UP × closeness per scan while the pair exists (full after ~3 min at closeness 1); closeness = 1 at d ≤ 60, falling linearly to 0.25 at 140 (really close pairs heat up 4× faster), × 2 while at-war armies of W's owner stand within COVER_R of V or anyone besieges W; decays T_DOWN per scan once the pair is gone (one owner). Map V -> T, V -> W. Logged in `strat` rows (top pair) and a `tension` row on change.

**Effects, in build order (each verified before the next):**
1. *Contest (built, `rules/tension.py` + hunt):* someone besieging W (neutral or ours to defend) next to V → contest hunt with entry ENTER → KEEP (1.5 → 1.0) as T goes 0 → 1; it runs even while our rally gathers elsewhere (the contact village is the rally's purpose). Never below KEEP; supply budget and turret rules unchanged.
2. *Take it (built):* Annex value × (1 + T) for a neutral W; the director's press treats an enemy W as soft at PRESS (3) → ENTER (1.5) as T goes 0 → 1 (applies to any village with tension; never dropped as a plain lone Annex candidate).
2b. *Gate (built):* vanilla lists an enemy's villages for Annex / Pillage only at desiredStatus ≥ 1 (its daily diplomatic wish, 0 toward most enemies). At war and T ≥ TEN_GATE (the cap) on a pair, that owner is listed for us, but the target scores keep only the partner village (its other villages stay unlisted); vanilla's own sizing and our launch gates decide whether we go. A launch on the partner that creates no order while we had an army to send (we're too weak there; wiped or all busy doesn't count) halves that pair's tension (TEN_FAIL): interest drops, other targets win, contact builds it back, at each faction's own pace.
3. *Truce (only after 1-2 are verified):* W's owner is in Peace (Truce) with us. Statuses are War / Peace (plus Tribute, Alliance); the "treaties" (trade, research, political agreement, open borders, ...) are separate agreements on top, cancelled for `Diplomacy_TreatyCancel_InfluenceCost` — not the truce. Ending the truce = declaring war (`DiplomacyManager.checkDeclareWar` → `declareWar`): one Influence cost `HDiplomacy.getWarDeclaringCosts` (base `Trading_DeclareWar_InfluenceCost` 50 × a factor: attributes 1178 instigator / 1177 victim, grows with our number of truces), refused within `Diplomacy_DiplomaticStatusChange_GracePeriod_Days` 10 of the last peace offering, with our units in their territory (`UnitsInEnemyTerritory`), a shuttle inbound, an ally or our tribute liege. Declaring war ends every treaty between the pair (`setDiplomaticStatus` War → `endTreaty`, except HarmlessGadget) and is no treason (`broken` false: victim alert "Worsen"). **Vanilla AI already declares war itself:** closure f@40799 (an `AIOrder` completion callback) on Success against a target whose owner is in Peace with us → `declareWar`; and `getSiegeableVillages` keeps a truce partner's village when `canDeclareWar`. So step 3 must not add a second declaration path: it only steers whether that target gets chosen. Attacking during a truce without declaring (treason, `Faction.onHostileAction`): the attacker pays `HDiplomacy.getDiplomaticStateChangeCosts` in **Standing** (`valueCache[295]` + victim's `Treaty_EnemyBreak_TResLoss_Flat` (Atreides −50 more), × attacker's `Treaty_Break_TResLoss_MRatio`, × `TRes_Lost_Used_TruceFaction_MRatio`^truces) and gets the timed Traitor trait (`valueCache[296]` days); skipped with attacker attribute `Treason_NoMalus` (1174; Bene Gesserit quest reward) or victim `Treason_NoMalusForTraitor` (1175). The victim always gets Betrayed (Ecaz: own traits via `overrideTreasonTraits`) and `OnBetray_TUnit_SpawnMainBase` units; status → War, event `TruceBroken_Traitor`. Default: no attack during a truce (betrayal penalties) — a default, not an invariant: later macro decisions (§5b) may override it. Fremen have no Standing at all (user), so treason costs them nothing beyond the victim's Betrayed trait and spawned units; declaring war would cost them Influence for nothing. Fremen may press at T ≥ 1 held T_HOLD s; others: once T ≥ 1 for T_HOLD s, `checkDeclareWar` is Success, B(owner) ≥ 1 and W is soft → let the target through (vanilla declares on launch), then the normal press. One per faction per long cooldown; logged.

**Guards:** T only scales thresholds that already exist (entry ratio, score, softness); no new orders except the diplomatic break; posture order (§5a) and supply rules still win; tension never makes a faction attack a stronger owner (B ≥ 1).

## 6. Code rules

- One concern per module: `aware.py` (world model + log), `rules/world.py` (shared queries), one `rules/` module per rule group, `behave.py` (wiring). Each rule is one function: read the model, decide, issue at most one order, log one event with a reason.
- All thresholds are named constants at the top of the module, from §4 where one applies. Move them to data (`constant` rows) once stable.
- Throttle: rules run in the aware scan (10 s per faction). Worst case O(armies²) per scan; nothing per tick.
- Reuse vanilla entry points (`addOrder`, `tryArmyAction`, `pickUnits`) so vanilla bookkeeping (reservations, phases, cancel) stays consistent.
- Offline check after every change: both boot files patch and re-parse, and a decompile of the new functions reads right. In game: `HEALTH OK`, then the targeted log metric.

## 7. Checklist for a new rule

1. Which logged failure does it fix, and which `mod log` number should change?
2. Which layer is it, and which priority?
3. Does it read the shared world model (no private scan)?
4. What are its enter / keep / abort values and triggers? Can it flip-flop with itself?
5. Can it loop with another rule or with vanilla (e.g. hunt → lose → retreat → hunt again)? What breaks the loop?
6. What happens if the target moves, disappears or runs home?
7. Does it concentrate force (no trickle)?
8. What does it log, and with which reason code?
9. If it throws or finds nothing, does vanilla behavior continue?
